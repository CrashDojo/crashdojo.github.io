00Abstract

A collision objective
is not a test.

CrashDojo picks who should cause the conflict, what maneuver it performs and how hard it pushes — from accident evidence — and then steers a frozen traffic world model to carry it out while the ego planner reacts.

Read the full abstract

Evaluating autonomous-driving planners requires safety-critical traffic interactions that are rarely encountered in recorded data. Guided traffic generators can induce collisions and adapt to ego responses, but a collision objective alone does not fully specify the interaction being tested: which actor initiates the conflict, what maneuver it performs, and how strongly it intervenes. Accident evidence can inform these choices, yet closed-loop execution introduces a further challenge: fixed trajectories cannot adapt to the ego response, while unconstrained replanning may alter the intended maneuver and its timing.

We present CrashDojo, which connects accident-grounded interaction selection to persistent request-guided control of a frozen traffic world model. Road structure, participant roles, and accident evidence rank candidate actor–mechanism requests, fixing the selected actor, maneuver parameters, and timing before rollout. Geometry-specific objectives guide target motion on the original request clock, while contextual motion references and observed ego–target proximity modulate contact pressure during execution. Candidate checks assess executable trajectory prefixes.

Across five planners on 100 nuScenes and 100 nuPlan scenes, CrashDojo matches or exceeds SAFE-SIM in initial-target contact in three of four dataset–planner groups while reducing target–background contact and target body off-road rates in all four. Motion-distribution comparisons and a separate three-seed component study further characterize the generated encounters.

01Motivation

Every crash test hides
three choices.

WHO

Which vehicle initiates the conflict?

The actor must be able to cause this kind of crash: same lane, adjacent lane, or a crossing route.

WHAT

What behavior does it execute?

An accident type becomes concrete targets: a lateral shift, a speed goal, an intrusion depth.

HOW HARD

How strongly should it intervene?

Driving logs set the intensity; pressure adapts online as the ego responds.

02Method

From accident context
to a planner challenge.

Fix the request offline. Guide it online, re-querying the ego planner every 0.5 s.

a

Lexicographic actor selection

Seven ordered checks rank each actor–mechanism candidate. Geometry never outweighs weaker accident evidence.

b

Geometry-specific references

Each mechanism has its own geometric target, followed on a fixed request clock.

c

Frozen flow-matching world model

The generator is never retrained; guidance acts only at sampling time.

Fine print,
up front.
